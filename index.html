Fractions

Year 6 maths: types of fractions, simplifying, adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing.

[[1/2]][[3/4]][[5/6]]

The parts of a fraction

A fraction shows part of a whole. The numerator (top number) says how many parts we have. The denominator (bottom number) says how many equal parts the whole is cut into.

{3/5} means 3 parts out of 5 equal parts.

[[3/5]]

Unit and non-unit fractions

A unit fraction has a numerator of 1, like {1/4} or {1/10}. It is just one equal part.

[[1/4]]

A non-unit fraction has a numerator bigger than 1. {3/4} is 3 lots of {1/4}.

[[3/4]]

The bigger the denominator, the smaller each part is. So {1/10} is smaller than {1/4}.

Proper, improper and mixed fractions

Proper fraction: the numerator is smaller than the denominator, so it is less than 1 whole, like {3/4}.

Improper fraction: the numerator is the same as or bigger than the denominator, so it is 1 whole or more, like {7/4}.

Mixed number: a whole number and a proper fraction together, like {1 3/4}.

[[7/4]]

{7/4} is 1 whole bar and 3 more quarters, so {7/4} = {1 3/4}.

Improper to mixed

Write {11/4} as a mixed number.

Divide the numerator by the denominator: 11 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 3.

2 is the number of wholes and 3 is the number of quarters left over. So {11/4} = {2 3/4}.

Mixed to improper

Write {3 2/5} as an improper fraction.

Multiply the whole number by the denominator: 3 × 5 = 15. Add the numerator: 15 + 2 = 17.

Keep the denominator. So {3 2/5} = {17/5}.

Simplifying fractions

A fraction is in its simplest form when no number except 1 goes into both the numerator and the denominator. Simplifying gives a fraction with smaller numbers but exactly the same value.

{6/9} and {2/3} are equivalent, but {2/3} is simpler. Both bars have the same amount shaded.

[[6/9]][[2/3]]

Divide the top and bottom by the same number

Simplify {12/18}. Both numbers are in the 6 times table, so divide the top and the bottom by 6: 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3.

{12/18} = {2/3}. The only number that goes into 2 and 3 is 1, so it is finished.

Highest common factor

Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. The biggest number in both lists is 6, so divide by 6 to simplify in one step.

If you cannot spot it, use small steps: {24/36} ÷ 2 = {12/18}, then ÷ 3 = {4/6}, then ÷ 2 = {2/3}.

Mixed numbers

Only simplify the fraction part. The whole number stays the same: {3 4/10} = {3 2/5}.

Equivalent fractions

To make an equivalent fraction, multiply the top and bottom by the same number: {1/2} = {2/4} = {3/6}. Simplifying is the same idea in reverse.

Common mistake: divide the top and the bottom by the same number. Changing only one of them changes the value.

Just starting addition?

Begin with fractions that have the same denominator. The start-here set has pictures, small numbers and full steps, and it builds up gently.

Same denominators: add and subtract

When the denominators are the same, the parts are the same size. So we only add or subtract the numerators. The denominator stays the same.

{3/8} + {2/8} = {5/8}

[[3/8]][[2/8]][[5/8]]

Common mistake: never add the denominators. {3/8} + {2/8} is not {5/16}.

Going over 1 whole

{5/6} + {4/6} = {9/6}. This is improper, so change it: 9 ÷ 6 = 1 remainder 3, so {9/6} = {1 3/6}.

Now simplify the fraction part: divide the top and bottom by 3. {3/6} = {1/2}. The answer is {1 1/2}.

Taking a fraction from a whole number

1 − {3/8}: write 1 as {8/8}. Then {8/8} − {3/8} = {5/8}.

2 − {3/8}: write 2 as {16/8}. Then {16/8} − {3/8} = {13/8} = {1 5/8}.

Different denominators

You can only add or subtract when the parts are the same size. So first change both fractions so they have a common denominator. This is a number that both denominators go into. The lowest common multiple is the easiest to use.

Add: {2/3} + {1/4}

Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. The lowest common denominator is 12.

{2/3} = {8/12} (multiply top and bottom by 4). {1/4} = {3/12} (multiply top and bottom by 3).

{8/12} + {3/12} = {11/12}

Subtract: {3/4} − {1/6}

The lowest common denominator is 12. {3/4} = {9/12} and {1/6} = {2/12}.

{9/12} − {2/12} = {7/12}

Shortcut

If one denominator is a multiple of the other, you only change one fraction. {1/2} + {1/8}: change {1/2} to {4/8}. Then {4/8} + {1/8} = {5/8}.

Mixed numbers

Adding: {2 3/4} + {1 1/2}

Add the wholes: 2 + 1 = 3. Add the fractions: {3/4} + {2/4} = {5/4} = {1 1/4}.

Add the extra whole: 3 + 1 = 4. The answer is {4 1/4}.

Subtracting: {3 1/3} − {1 5/6}

The fraction {1/3} is smaller than {5/6}, so it is tricky to subtract the parts separately. Change both to improper fractions.

{3 1/3} = {10/3} and {1 5/6} = {11/6}. Make the denominators match: {10/3} = {20/6}.

{20/6} − {11/6} = {9/6} = {1 3/6} = {1 1/2}

Just starting to multiply fractions?

Begin with unit fractions and small whole numbers. The start-here set has pictures and full steps.

Fraction × whole number

{3/5} × 4 means 4 lots of {3/5}: {3/5} + {3/5} + {3/5} + {3/5} = {12/5}.

[[12/5]]

The quick way: multiply the numerator by the whole number and keep the denominator the same.

{3/5} × 4: 3 × 4 = 12, so the answer is {12/5}. Change to a mixed number: 12 ÷ 5 = 2 remainder 2, so {12/5} = {2 2/5}.

Mixed number × whole number

{2 1/3} × 3. Change to an improper fraction first: {2 1/3} = {7/3}.

Multiply the numerator: 7 × 3 = 21. So {7/3} × 3 = {21/3} = 7.

Another way: 2 × 3 = 6 and {1/3} × 3 = 1, so 6 + 1 = 7.

Fraction × fraction

{1/2} × {1/4} means half of a quarter, which is {1/8}.

Rule: multiply the numerators, then multiply the denominators.

{2/3} × {3/5}: 2 × 3 = 6 and 3 × 5 = 15, so {6/15}. Simplify by dividing the top and bottom by 3: {2/5}.

Cancel first

In {2/3} × {3/5}, the 3 on the bottom of one fraction and the 3 on the top of the other can both be divided by 3. That leaves {2/1} × {1/5} = {2/5}, with smaller numbers to work with.

Fraction of an amount

"Of" means multiply. To find {3/4} of 20: divide by the denominator, 20 ÷ 4 = 5. Then multiply by the numerator, 5 × 3 = 15.

Just starting to divide fractions?

Begin with sharing a unit fraction into equal parts. The start-here set has pictures and full steps.

Dividing a fraction by a whole number

{1/3} ÷ 2 means sharing one third into 2 equal parts. Each part is a sixth, so {1/3} ÷ 2 = {1/6}.

[[1/3]][[1/6]]

Rule: keep the numerator and multiply the denominator by the whole number.

{3/4} ÷ 2: the denominator is 4 × 2 = 8, so the answer is {3/8}.

{4/5} ÷ 2: the denominator is 5 × 2 = 10, so {4/10}. Simplify by dividing by 2: {2/5}.

Check with the inverse

Multiplying undoes dividing. {3/8} × 2 = {6/8} = {3/4}, which matches what we started with.

Finding the whole

If {1/4} of a length is 36 cm, then the whole length is 4 times as long: 36 × 4 = 144 cm.

Sharing problems

{1/2} of a pizza is shared equally between 3 people. Each person gets {1/2} ÷ 3 = {1/6} of the whole pizza.

Extension: dividing by a fraction

This goes beyond the Year 6 SATs, but it is a good challenge. Dividing asks: how many times does it fit?

Whole number ÷ unit fraction. How many {1/4}s are in 3? Each whole has 4 quarters, so 3 ÷ {1/4} = 3 × 4 = 12.

[[4/4]]

Same denominators. {6/8} ÷ {3/8}: both are counted in eighths, so just divide the numerators: 6 ÷ 3 = 2.

Different denominators: keep, flip, multiply. Keep the first fraction, flip the second one upside down, and change ÷ to ×.

{2/3} ÷ {1/6} = {2/3} × {6/1} = {12/3} = 4.

{3/4} ÷ {3/8} = {3/4} × {8/3} = {24/12} = 2.

Dividing by a fraction smaller than 1 makes the answer bigger. That is because more of the small pieces fit in.

SATs tips

1. Read how the answer should be given: "simplest form" or "mixed number".

2. To simplify, divide the top and bottom by the same number until you cannot go any further.

3. Check your answer makes sense. If you add two fractions that are each more than a half, the answer must be more than 1.

4. "What fraction is left?" Work out what has been used, then take it away from 1 whole.

5. Fraction of an amount: divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator. {3/8} of £40: 40 ÷ 8 = 5, then 5 × 3 = £15.
